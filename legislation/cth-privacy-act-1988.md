# Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)

## Relevance to Module 9 Workbook 1

Workbook 1 section 4.1 names the Privacy Act 1988 in connection with using personal information for real-estate marketing. Whether a particular agency is an `APP entity`, and which exceptions apply, must be checked from the Act and the facts.

## Schedule 1 — Australian Privacy Principles (selected provisions)

### APP 3 — Collection of solicited personal information

For an organisation, APP 3.2 permits collection of non-sensitive personal information only where it is reasonably necessary for one or more of the organisation’s functions or activities. APP 3.3 restricts collection of sensitive information unless an exception applies, including the specified consent-and-necessity condition.

### APP 5 — Notification of collection

APP 5.1 requires reasonable steps, at or before collection (or as soon as practicable after), to notify the individual of relevant APP 5.2 matters or otherwise ensure that they are aware. These matters include identity/contact details, source where relevant, collection purpose, usual disclosures, access/correction information and complaint process.

### APP 6 — Use or disclosure

APP 6.1 generally prevents an APP entity from using or disclosing personal information collected for a primary purpose for another purpose, unless consent or a listed exception applies.

### APP 7 — Direct marketing

APP 7.1 states that an organisation holding personal information must not use or disclose it for direct marketing, subject to APP 7 exceptions. For non-sensitive information, APP 7.2 and 7.3 include conditions concerning collection source and reasonable expectation or consent/impracticability, a simple opt-out means, notice of that means where required, and no existing opt-out request.

### APP 11 — Security of personal information

APP 11.1 requires reasonable steps to protect held personal information from misuse, interference, loss, unauthorised access, modification or disclosure. APP 11.2 requires reasonable steps to destroy or de-identify information no longer needed, subject to its conditions.

> The Workbook’s simple wording about permission and notifying people affected by a breach is not a complete statement of the Privacy Act. Use the official PDF and the relevant legal test if an Assessment asks for a current-law answer.
