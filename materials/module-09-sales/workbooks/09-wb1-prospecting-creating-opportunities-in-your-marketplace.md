# Prospecting: Creating Opportunities in Your Marketplace

> Platform title: **Appraise List Cluster (Sales) – Workbook 1**. The lesson overview describes this workbook as *Prospecting: Creating Opportunities in Your Marketplace*.

## Section 1. Foundations of effective prospecting

### Overview

**As a real estate sales representative, prospecting is one of the most important things you’ll do.** It’s how you create opportunities, build relationships, and consistently secure listings for your agency.

Without listings, there’s nothing to promote, sell, or lease—so prospecting is the foundation of your workload and your future success.

![REIWA - WIDE (8).png](https://fs-prd.axcelerate.com.au/images/66949502/9UeltRHFO9FnJ5sQBBEaUlJqb/REIWA%20-%20WIDE%20(8).png)

### 1.1 What you’ll be doing as a sales representative

**One of your core responsibilities is to prospect for listings.** That means actively promoting yourself in the community, building trust with potential sellers, and staying visible and helpful.

During this stage of the sales process, you’ll:

- Create a prospecting plan that fits your market and brand.
- Put your plan into action to start generating leads.
- Discover what sellers really need and expect from the selling process.
- Track your results so you can learn what’s working.
- Stay in touch with prospective sellers and keep the relationship alive.

Throughout it all, you’ll need to work in line with your agency’s policies, comply with current legislation, and always uphold professional and ethical standards. That’s what sets trusted agents apart.

![Gina---REIWA---WIDE--82-.png](https://fs-prd.axcelerate.com.au/images/66949502/Gina---REIWA---WIDE--82-.png)

### 1.2 Key concepts

**What is a listing?**

A listing is a property that your agency has been formally appointed to market and manage. This appointment comes through a listing agreement, which is a legally binding contract between the seller (or lessor) and the agency.

Your job is to secure listings on behalf of your agency—and quality listings are one of the most valuable things you can bring to the team.

**What is a 'farm area'?**

In most agencies, you’ll be assigned a prospecting area, often called your farm. This is the local area you’ll get to know deeply—building relationships, monitoring sales activity, and becoming a trusted name.

Your approach to prospecting in your farm will depend on:

- local market conditions
- your agency’s policies and tools
- your own strengths and confidence
- the level of competition.

Your goal is to become the go-to local expert when someone is ready to sell or lease.

### 1.3 Why you need a prospecting plan

**Successful prospecting doesn’t happen by luck—it’s planned.** Having a strategy helps you:

- stay consistent, even when busy
- focus on high-impact activities (rather than just staying “busy”)
- build your brand and niche over time
- reduce compliance and ethical risks
- track your growth and learn from experience.

When you’re just starting out, prospecting will take up more of your time—you’re still building your name, database, and reputation. That’s normal. A strong plan helps you use that time well.

![REIWA - WIDE (4).png](https://fs-prd.axcelerate.com.au/images/66949502/ipCAuWRrAfiiH6J3cm5zuoMKI/REIWA%20-%20WIDE%20(4).png)

### 1.4 Creating your prospecting plan

Your prospecting plan doesn’t need to be complicated—but it does need to be clear. It should answer these core questions:

- Who are you targeting?
- What methods will you use?
- How often will you prospect?
- How will you keep track?

### 1.5 Build your brand, build your business

**Real estate is a relationship business.** Prospecting isn't about “hard selling”—it’s about making connections, understanding local needs, and offering helpful, professional service.

Over time, people in your farm area will come to know you, trust you, and refer others to you. That’s how listings start to come in more easily—and why early, consistent prospecting is so valuable.

![REIWA - WIDE (3).png](https://fs-prd.axcelerate.com.au/images/66949502/YtYkAL7p3bTAfs6o5ZCXy3fLo/REIWA%20-%20WIDE%20(3).png)

### 1.6 Legal and ethical reminders

While prospecting, always follow:

- The Do Not Call Register (DNCR).
- The REBA Code of Conduct (e.g. don’t solicit a seller under exclusive agency).
- Australian Consumer Law (don’t mislead or make false promises).
- Your agency’s internal policies.

### 1.7 Case studies

#### First-year sales representative – Building momentum with a prospecting plan

**Background**

Amelia joined Sparkling Waters Realty six months ago, eager but nervous about finding her first listing. Her licensee encouraged her to create a simple prospecting plan focusing on her assigned farm area.

**Action taken**

- Amelia blocked out two hours each morning for prospecting.
- She delivered local market update flyers, knocked on doors to introduce herself, and made follow-up calls to people who attended home opens.
- She tracked every contact in the agency CRM, marking them as hot, warm, or cold leads.
- She stayed consistent, even when it felt discouraging, because she knew relationships take time.

**Results**

Three months in, a couple she’d met while door knocking called her back. They weren’t ready to sell when she first visited, but her friendly follow-ups and market updates kept her front of mind. Amelia appraised their home, won their trust, and secured her very first exclusive listing.

A few weeks later, she converted another lead from her flyer drop into a listing. Both clients mentioned that they chose her because she was “persistent without being pushy” and “always seemed prepared.”

**Learning point**

By sticking to her plan and tracking her progress, Amelia proved that consistent prospecting works—even for a brand-new representative without a track record. Her confidence grew alongside her database.

#### Experienced sales representative – Staying relevant with a prospecting plan

**Background**

David has been selling real estate for over 12 years and is a well-known face in the Sparkling Waters area. With a large past client base, it could be easy for him to rely solely on referrals. Instead, he still runs a structured prospecting plan to protect his pipeline.

**Actions taken**

- David schedules quarterly market update emails and personalised anniversary cards to past clients.
- He sponsors a local sporting club and regularly attends their events, using it as a soft-touch way to stay visible.
- He reviews his CRM each week and makes 10 targeted calls—checking in on past buyers, investors, and anyone who previously requested an appraisal.
- He uses social media advertising with geo-targeting to remind locals that he is active and approachable.

**Results**

Recently, one of David’s long-time contacts reached out after receiving his quarterly email. They weren’t planning to sell, but a job opportunity interstate changed their plans. Because David had kept in touch for years, they didn’t consider anyone else.

That same month, his call to an investor client resulted in a referral to a neighbour looking to downsize. Both contacts turned into listings, adding to David’s already strong year.

**Learning point**

Even experienced agents can’t rely solely on reputation. A structured prospecting plan keeps David relevant, protects him from market downturns, and ensures that referrals and repeat business continue to flow.

## Section 2. Formulating a plan

### Overview

**Purpose of a plan**

A strong prospecting plan helps you:

- find leads and build your database
- stay focused and consistent
- manage risk and remain compliant
- set yourself up for success—now and in the future.

Think long-term. That flyer you deliver today, or the phone call you make this week, could turn into a listing in six months or a referral in a year. Every single contact matters. Consistent prospecting across your career is what builds longevity and keeps opportunities flowing.

![REIWA 1.png](https://fs-prd.axcelerate.com.au/images/66949502/REIWA-1--7-.png)

### 2.1 Steps to follow

1. Know your ideal customer.
2. Set clear weekly goals.
3. Block out prospecting time.
4. Create your prospecting script.
5. Quality your leads with a checklist.
6. Use technology to help you.
7. Act fast on every lead.
8. Stay in touch with past clients.
9. Review and improve every year.

### 2.2 Choose the best prospecting methods

**Prospecting is most effective when it’s targeted and intentional.** That means matching your method to your market and your strengths.

Whether you're starting out or already building momentum, the best sales reps are always refining their prospecting strategy based on what works. Below is a breakdown of current best practices across key prospecting channels.

**What the data shows: What drives listings?**

- Referrals and repeat business are the top performers because they’re built on trust.
- Direct prospecting (calls, flyers, and home opens) generates steady opportunities when done consistently.
- Digital marketing such as online ads and social media increases visibility and helps you stay front of mind.
- Other channels like print media, signage, and walk-ins play a smaller supporting role—but they still add credibility and community presence.

**Key takeaway:** Your database, your reputation, and your ability to follow up are your greatest assets.

**Best practice prospecting channels**

- Referrals and repeat business (top perfomancers)
- Your CRM and database
- Home opens and recently sold properties
- Website enquiries
- Print ads and local promotion
- Signboards, shopfronts and walk-ins
- Social media and digital prospecting
- Community involvement
- Friends, family and neighbours
- Door knocking and cold calling
- Building sites and council records
- Off-the-plan listings
- Letterbox cards and direct mail
- Telephone enquiries and general calls
- Private sellers ('for sale by owner')

## Section 3. Measuring prospecting success

### Overview

**Every prospecting method can bring results.** But some are far more effective than others depending on your market, your strategy, and how well you follow through.

To maximise your success, it’s essential to track what’s working—and what’s not—so you can use your time and resources wisely.

![Gina---REIWA---WIDE--86-.png](https://fs-prd.axcelerate.com.au/images/66949502/Gina---REIWA---WIDE---2025-11-07T111123.302.png)

### 3.1 How to measure what’s working

Here are some practical ways to measure the effectiveness of your prospecting plan.

| Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Number of new contacts added | Is your database growing? A bigger, well-managed database supports future sales. |
| Cost per lead / cost per listing | Are your marketing efforts delivering value? E.g. $200 Facebook campaign = $66/lead. |
| Website conversion rate | Are your visitors turning into real leads? 1,000 visits + 10 leads = 1% conversion. |
| Sales growth over time | Are listings and sales increasing? This shows whether your overall approach is working. |

Tip: Focus on meaningful metrics. For example, likes on a post look good, but leads in your CRM are what actually build your business.

**Using digital metrics**

Most digital platforms (like Facebook Ads or Google Analytics) come with built-in tools that help you track:

- ad impressions and clicks
- engagement (likes, comments, shares)
- conversions (form fills, appraisals booked)
- demographics reached.

Make sure your campaigns are set up to track the outcomes that matter—not just vanity metrics.

![REIWA 1.png](https://fs-prd.axcelerate.com.au/images/66949502/REIWA-1--3-(723E8D7C-C7B1-4AC4-B1F6-8A103AB1EDBE).png)

### 3.2 Once you’ve gathered results

**Focus your energy.** Ask:

- Which methods are bringing in qualified leads?
- Which strategies cost the least but deliver strong results?
- What tactics seem promising but need refining?
- What’s not working—and should be dropped or adjusted?

This kind of reflection will make your future prospecting far more effective.

**Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)**

Most agencies will track your prospecting performance using KPIs. These help you stay focused and make sure you're progressing steadily.

| KPI | Timeframe |
|---|---|
| Number of appraisals conducted | Weekly / Fortnightly / Monthly |
| Number of contacts made with new prospects | Weekly / Fortnightly / Monthly |
| Number of listing presentations delivered | Weekly / Fortnightly / Monthly |
| Number of new listings secured | Weekly / Fortnightly / Monthly |

Tracking your performance against these KPIs can help you stay motivated, identify patterns, and celebrate your wins.

## Section 4. Stay legal and ethical in your prospecting

### Overview

**Prospecting is essential to your success.** It must always be done legally, ethically, and in line with your agency’s policies and current legislation.

![REIWA - WIDE (11).png](https://fs-prd.axcelerate.com.au/images/66949502/FvBGuThIPCuJKjAa4fUKylaEb/REIWA%20-%20WIDE%20(11).png)

### 4.1 Key legal frameworks

**Here’s what you need to know to stay compliant and protect yourself and your agency.**

#### REBA Code of conduct (2016)

**Rule 22: Inducing a breach Is prohibited.** You must not knowingly encourage or attempt to:

- break a current listing agreement
- breach a contract of sale or lease
- create a situation where a seller might owe commission to multiple agents.

Examples of prohibited actions:

- Calling or emailing a seller whose property is exclusively listed with another agency.
- Leaving a letterbox card or knocking on their door.

**Good practice:** Only respond to unsolicited enquiries from a seller in another agency’s care—and never take on a listing unless you’ve confirmed their existing contract has legally expired or been terminated in writing.

**Rule 16(4): Referral disclosure:** If you receive a referral fee, you must disclose this in writing to the client before being appointed.

#### Avoid conflicts of interest

While rare in prospecting, a conflict can occur if:

- You’re listing multiple similar properties in the same strata or development.
- One seller (like a developer) is a regular client, creating a perception of bias.

**Best practice:**

- Disclose the situation to both sellers.
- Be transparent about confidentiality and fair service.

#### Australian Consumer Law (ACL) and cold contacting

If you approach a prospect before they approach you, the ACL treats this as an unsolicited consumer contract. Strict rules apply.

**When you can’t cold call:**

| Time | Rule |
|---|---|
| Sundays or Public Holidays | Never |
| Weekdays | Before 9am or after 8pm |
| Saturdays | Before 9am or after 5pm |

If you knock or call:

- identify yourself and your agency clearly
- state the purpose of your visit/call
- leave immediately if asked, and do not recontact for at least 30 days
- provide written information about cooling-off rights
- do not advertise or show the property, or accept any payment, for 10 business days.

#### 'Do Not Call' Register (DNCR)

It is illegal to make telemarketing calls to numbers listed on the DNCR unless the person has given explicit permission.

**Good practice:**

- Check numbers before calling.
- Keep records of “do not contact” requests.
- Never call someone again after they’ve asked you not to.

#### Spam Act 2003

This legislation protects consumers from unwanted digital messages, including:

- emails
- SMS or MMS
- instant messaging.

**What’s required:**

- Consent before sending messages.
- Clear identification of you and your agency.
- A working “unsubscribe” link.
- Immediate action on unsubscribe requests.

Penalties for agencies can exceed $1 million per day for repeat offences.

#### Privacy Act 1988

If your agency holds personal information, it must:

- only use it with the person’s permission (implied or express)
- keep it secure and confidential
- inform anyone affected by a breach.

**Good practice:**

- Don’t add people to marketing databases without consent.
- Secure contact info—digital or physical.

#### Confidentiality obligations (Common Law)

Your duty of confidentiality doesn’t start when someone signs with you—it starts the moment they consider it.

Even if they don’t become a client, you must:

- not share their details
- not disclose personal circumstances
- treat their enquiry as confidential.

This is both a legal and ethical obligation.

## Section 5. Closing note

### Prospecting as a career foundation

Prospecting is more than a task—it’s the foundation of a successful sales career. By staying consistent, building genuine relationships, and keeping your database at the heart of your strategy, you’ll create opportunities that last well beyond your first listing.

Remember: every conversation, flyer, or follow-up is a step toward future business.

Use what you’ve learned here to refine your approach, stay compliant, and position yourself as a trusted professional in your marketplace. Longevity in real estate isn’t built overnight—it’s built through steady, deliberate prospecting.
