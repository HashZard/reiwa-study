# Choosing the Right Appraisal Method

## Section 1. Appraisal methods – Overview

What is the best appraisal method to use?
Once you've confirmed the property owner and verified the type of title or tenure, your next step is to choose the right appraisal method. The method you choose will shape the type of research and analysis you need to carry out.
Use industry-accepted methods
The real estate industry recognises several appraisal methods, but your choice should always be:
Suitable for the property type and context
	
Supported by reliable, current data
	
Defensible with evidence (especially if pricing is questioned later)

## 1.1 Comparative Market Analysis (CMA)

The CMA is the most common method
The Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) method is the industry standard for appraising:
Residential properties.
Vacant land.
Rental properties (for calculating Estimated Rental Value or ERV).
It works by directly comparing the subject property with:
recently sold comparable properties (for sales)
currently advertised rentals (for leases).
Most agencies subscribe to real estate databases that allow fast access to this information and provide tools to automate CMA reports.
CMA is reliable, current, and widely accepted—especially in markets where there's enough recent, local data to support your price opinion.
Important: If fewer than three comparable properties are available, use a second appraisal method to support your price estimate.
	
Scenario: When CMA works best
Amir was asked to appraise a three-bedroom home in a suburb with plenty of recent sales.
Using a CMA, he selected four similar properties that had sold in the past three months.
His price range was clear, realistic, and easy to explain to the owners.
They appreciated the transparency and listed with his agency the following week.
Learning point: In areas with strong, recent sales evidence, a CMA provides reliable data and builds client trust.

## 1.2 Alternative methods

When CMA isn’t enough
Sometimes, the property or market conditions mean a CMA alone isn’t appropriate. In these cases, alternative methods may be used though they come with extra complexity and risk.
Summation method (Land + Improvements)
Depreciated replacement cost (DRC)
Return on Investment (ROI)
​ 
Scenario: When you need another method
Sophie was asked to appraise a unique, architect-designed home on a large semi-rural block.
She found only one remotely comparable sale in the past year.
To support her CMA, Sophie also used the Summation Method, adding the estimated land value to the cost of improvements.
She explained the limitations of this approach and recommended the owners seek a formal valuation if they needed a figure for finance.
Learning point: Not every property fits the CMA method alone. Knowing when to use a secondary method—and when to refer to a valuer—demonstrates professionalism and reduces risk.

## 1.3 Comparables

What information is used in direct comparison and what property attributes affect price?
Zoning, title and other influences on property value
Zoning and development potential
Rental zoning considerations

Title or tenure: Freehold vs strata
When comparing properties, title type matters, especially for sales appraisals.
Title type
	
Effect on appraisal


Green Title
	
Usually commands a premium due to land size and no strata fees


Strata Title
	
Typically valued slightly lower due to shared ownership and levies


Survey Strata
	
Often seen as a hybrid—less land than green title, but more independent than strata
Best practice:
Compare like with like—strata with strata, green title with green title.
Where that’s not possible, adjust for land area, fees, and buyer preferences.

What about rental appraisals?
For rentals, title matters less—tenants are more concerned with property condition, amenities and location than legal ownership structure.
The direct comparison method is also the go-to approach for rental appraisals. While some alternatives exist, such as:
rate per square metre (used in commercial properties)
Landgate rental values (mass appraisal figures, not current).
	
These are less relevant in residential property management and don’t reflect current leasing conditions.

Tenancy status
A property’s tenancy can affect its market value, especially for sales.
Tenancy type
	
Impact on value


Vacant possession (residential)
	
More attractive to owner-occupiers → generally higher value


Tenanted (residential)
	
May reduce buyer pool to investors only → possible price adjustment


Tenanted (commercial)
	
Typically increases value due to income certainty and tenant-paid outgoings

Always consider the target buyer or investor. What appeals to them will influence the sale price.

‘Market’ value vs special cases
	
Not every sale or rental represents true market conditions. Avoid using comparables where:
the transaction was between family members
it involved related parties or companies
it was a forced or mortgagee sale
the result appears to deviate significantly from area trends.
If a price looks unusually high or low, do your due diligence.
Check the sale record.
Look at photos.
Search for public announcements (e.g. mortgagee auctions).
Ask local agents or colleagues.
The more you know your local market, the easier it is to spot outliers.

## 1.4 Final tip

Choose the method that is fit for purpose
Appraisal method
	
Best used for


CMA
	
Residential sales and rentals with recent local comparables


Summation
	
Unique or remote properties with no nearby sales


DRC
	
Special-use or depreciated buildings (use expert advice)


ROI
	
Investment property analysis (by buyer or adviser, not agent)
​ 
Always ensure your appraisal is:
Evidence-based
	
Clearly documented
	
Honest, realistic, and compliant
Know your local market
To improve your appraisal accuracy:
attend local home opens
review recent online listings and photos
stay informed about zoning changes and infrastructure updates
regularly update your local property knowledge.

## Section 2. Sourcing information for an appraisal – Overview

Appraising property accurately relies on using current, reliable data and knowing where to find it
Most agents use subscription platforms like Cotality (RP Data) or Pricefinder to prepare Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) reports. These systems pull property data directly from multiple sources and let you customise your search and reporting options.
You’ll learn to use these platforms once you're working in an agency. They’re designed to be intuitive and user-friendly.

## Section 3. Conclusion

Selecting the right appraisal method is more than a technical exercise—it’s a critical part of providing reliable, evidence-based advice to clients
By combining accurate property and market data with an appropriate method, and by understanding zoning, tenure, tenancy, and legal considerations, you can produce appraisals that are realistic, defensible, and compliant. Always document your approach, explain any assumptions clearly, and refer complex or high-risk properties to qualified valuers when necessary. With these skills, you’ll strengthen client trust, reduce risk, and enhance your professional reputation in every appraisal you conduct.
